Red lines — what never goes into Claude (or any public AI tool)

  • Patient names, phone numbers, or registers that identify people
  • Passwords, API keys, or credentials of any kind
  • Unpublished strategy documents or confidential drafts

Safe: published guidelines, public ESPEN data, anonymised aggregate numbers, your own drafts.

A weak answer

"Data management is a big challenge in our programme. Reports are often late and there are many errors. AI can help us improve data quality and efficiency."

Which data? Which report? How late? Nothing here to build a project on.

A strong answer (abridged)

"Task: compile 38 district MDA treatment files into the national annual report — Excel files, WhatsApp photos, phone calls. It takes my data officer and me about three weeks a year. Last year one district reported 112% coverage and I only caught it after the chart went to a partner; we sent a correction and missed the deadline by three weeks."

A named task, real occasions, numbers, consequences — we can build on this.

Think minimum viable product

Your problem statement does not have to be perfect — it has to work, and you have to understand why and how it works. We sharpen it every single class; each one gets you one step closer to a solution.

5 · The art of the perfect prompt

Before any tool matters, this matters. AI does not read your mind — vague in, vague out. Think of Claude as a brilliant new colleague who has never seen your programme: it needs direction, context, and a clear definition of "done".

P

Persona

Who is asking? Give the AI an expert perspective to think from.

"You are an NTD monitoring & evaluation officer…"

T

Task

What exactly should be done? One clear verb: summarize, compare, draft, check.

"Summarize coverage by district for 2025."

C

Context

Why and for whom? Audience, purpose, constraints, what data you have.

"For the national programme review; aggregate district data only."

F

Format

How should it look? Table, bullets, one page, formal French…

"A table plus three bullets, formal."

I

Iterate

Was it right? Evaluate, refine, run again. Good prompting is a loop, not a wish.

"Flag coverage over 100%; ask me before finalizing."

A worked example — colour-coded

Spot the parts: persona → task → context → format → iterate. Still room to improve — "easy-to-read format" is vague. Say what columns you want.

Same request, two prompts — see the difference

Three phrases worth memorizing

  • "Think through this with me step by step."
  • "That's helpful, but I need it specific to my context: …"
  • "Ask me questions to clarify my goal and surface my assumptions — don't decide for me."

Could we build a skill for the perfect prompt?

    
Yes — and that is exactly the plan. A skill is a saved, reusable set of instructions you can call on demand instead of retyping. The builder above is the draft of that skill: the P-T-C-F-I anatomy plus your house rules. Today you learn it by hand.

    
In Class 2 we turn your best prompts into a real skill, hunt for existing skills (they are shared, for example, on GitHub), and you learn to tell reusable prompts from one-offs. Homework prep: bring one task you repeat every week — that is your first skill candidate.

Quick answers

  
What if I miss a live class?
Watch the recording in the module channel, do the homework anyway, and ask on Slack. Don't disappear — the course moves every two weeks.

  
What if Claude gives me something wrong?
Treat it as a prompt problem first: iterate (the I in P-T-C-F-I is you). Verify every number against a source. If it keeps happening, post the prompt and output on Slack — debugging prompts is a group sport.

  
Can I work in French?
Yes. Write to Claude and on Slack in whichever language you think in; instructors read both. (This page toggles with EN/FR at the top.)

  
Where do I find everything?
One place: Slack. Slides, templates, recordings, and homework feedback all live there — this page is the only exception.
