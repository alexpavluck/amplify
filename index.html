Learn AI on a problem you own.

AMPLIFY 2027 is an eight-session course for staff of national NTD programmes. You bring one real task from your own work. Every session sharpens it or builds the AI skill to solve it, and you finish by presenting the solution.

Your problemat the centre of every session

Setup & AI fundamentals

Month 1 · Foundations · first session

You meet the cohort and the instructors, and leave with your own Claude project and a place on the course Slack. We introduce the vocabulary you will use for the next four months: what the different models are good at, the red lines on what must never go into an AI tool, and the habit of judging every answer, then keeping it, trying a new idea or discarding it.

Before the next session, you introduce yourself on Slack, post your problem for the whole cohort to read, and respond to at least two other people’s. By the end of the week your problem has a name, and other people are already thinking about it.

Prompting & skills

Month 1 · Foundations · second session

This session is about asking well. You learn how an effective prompt is put together, how to get answers that sound like you rather than a chatbot, and how to build a reusable skill or borrow one someone else has made. Most of the time is spent practising in small groups.

Afterwards, you turn those prompts on your own problem, using the AI to question it until it is sharper than the version you posted. You look for a skill that fits, and start to separate the parts of your task that repeat, which a skill can take on, from the steps you only do once.

Knowledge management

Month 2 · Knowledge & connectors · first session

Programme knowledge has a habit of disappearing. It leaves with staff, or sits in email threads nobody can search. You see how a notebook can hold it in one place, build one yourself in a hands-on session, and watch the instructor take a notebook’s synthesis into Claude to work with.

Your homework is a notebook built around your own problem: the reports, guidelines and past data it depends on, gathered where you and the AI can both use them.

Connectors to your data

Month 2 · Knowledge & connectors · second session

Next, the AI goes to where your information already lives. You connect Claude to tools you use every day, such as Google Drive and email, and to ESPEN data through a custom connector, then try each one on real examples.

Before the next session, you map which connectors reach the data and documents behind your problem. From here on, you are working with live information rather than copies.

AI for decision-making

Month 3 · Decisions & content · first session

Data only matters if it changes a decision. This session introduces a data-to-action framework: the decision you need to make, the source that informs it, the trigger that should prompt it, and the action that follows. You work through examples for each problem area, in groups with people tackling similar problems.

You then use AI to build a framework for your own project and run it, which puts a clear decision at the centre of your problem.

Documents & presentations

Month 3 · Decisions & content · second session

Now you make the case. You use AI to draft reports, checklists and presentations, then learn to improve on the first draft it hands you, with clearer visuals, useful diagrams, a sensible layout and attention to accessibility.

Your homework is the pitch for your solution, which becomes the first draft of your final presentation.

Presentations & dashboards

Month 4 · Visualisation & showcase · first session

The showcase begins, with the first five pairs presenting their solutions to the cohort. The second half turns to charting, and to knowing when a static dashboard is enough and when your audience needs a live one.

If you have not presented yet, you spend the next fortnight getting your presentation ready.

Presentations, maps & close

Month 4 · Visualisation & showcase · second session

The remaining pairs present, a guest speaker leads a session on maps, and the instructors close with a final worked demo. You leave with a solution you have presented to your peers.

Your last piece of work is a one-page reflection on what the course was worth to you, and on the blind spots that remain.

Start with the task, not the AI.

Your application begins with a problem statement. It asks about your work, not about AI: one specific task you do again and again, described as if a new colleague were taking it over next month. Everything you build in the course grows out of it.

  • Data management and analysisYou compile, clean, check or analyse programme data, whether it is numbers or interview notes.
  • CommunicationYou write reports, prepare slides, or run social and behaviour change campaigns.
  • Knowledge managementYou bring information together from different places and get it to the people who need it, when they need it, including through training.
  • Project managementYou plan, track and coordinate the work itself.

A weak problem statement

“Data management is a big challenge in our programme. Reports are often late and there are many errors. AI can help us improve data quality and efficiency.”

Which data? Which report? What errors? How late? Nobody could design a project from it.

A strong one

Task
I compile district MDA treatment data into a presentation, with charts and maps, for the national LF and oncho technical meetings.
How often
Once a year. It takes me and a data officer about three weeks.
Last time
After the 2025 round, 38 districts sent their summaries: 25 as Excel files by email, 9 as WhatsApp photos of paper forms, and 4 by phone. Several used old versions of the template, and I retyped every figure into our national workbook.
What went wrong
One district reported more people treated than lived there, which gave 112% coverage. I only noticed when I made the chart, after the numbers had already gone to a partner.
Better looks like
Every district’s data checked for obvious errors within a few days of arriving, and the report finished in one week instead of three.
