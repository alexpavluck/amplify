The shift underway

Two forces are closing in on national programmes at once.

Funding ↓

External support is contracting

As donor technical assistance draws down, the analytical work partners used to absorb lands squarely on Ministry of Health teams — permanently, not for the life of one grant.

Integration ↑

National systems raise the bar

Working inside DHIS2, HMIS and integrated surveillance means cleaning messy exports, reconciling sources and defending a recommendation with evidence — routinely, not once a year.

The honest response isn't a faster tool. It's sustainability.

Every programme eventually faces the same question: when the implementing partner's contract ends, does the capability leave with them?

Sustainability has to be designed in from the start — independent of how long any single implementation runs. A gap filled by borrowed hands is a gap that reopens. The durable fix is capability a country owns.

The capacity gap

Deep in the disease. Thin on the systems.

National NTD teams hold decades of hard-won domain knowledge — epidemiology, mass drug administration, vector control, the realities of reaching the last mile.

What they were never resourced or trained to carry is the other half: the information-systems and analytic skills that turn integrated, interoperable data into decisions a programme can stand behind.

That gap is where programmes stall — good data sitting unused, decisions made on last year's numbers, recommendations that can't be defended.

Build capacity, not dependency

A tool is a dot. Capacity is the whole loop.

It is tempting to answer the gap by handing a programme a tool — a chatbot, a dashboard, a custom model. It demos beautifully and solves exactly the problem you had the day it was built.

Then the indicator changes, the export breaks, the grant ends — and the programme waits for a vendor who may not come.

Building a tool is not the same as building the capacity to decide a tool is even needed, specify it, ship an MVP, deploy it, and improve it from real use. AMPLIFY teaches the loop, not the dot.

What AMPLIFY does

One programme, three things at once.

AMPLIFY is domain-specific by design — every exercise runs on NTD work, so there's no translation step between learning AI and applying it.

01

Country ownership

Capability that stays inside the Ministry — no vendor lock-in, no dependency on who holds this year's grant.

02

AI up-skilling

Practical fluency with modern AI tools, taught on the data and decisions fellows already own.

03

Team augmentation

NTD expertise paired with new data and systems skills — the two halves integration requires, in the same people.

Why six months learn → apply → mentored, not a one-week workshop
MONTHS 1–2

Learn

Foundations in AI fluency, prompting and data literacy — grounded in real NTD datasets, not sample spreadsheets.

MONTHS 3–4

Apply

Fellows take the skills into their own programmes — cleaning exports, building visualisations, turning field reports into action plans.

MONTHS 5–6

Mentored

Guided practice with feedback, so the skills hold after the cohort ends and travel to the rest of the team.

Cohort 1 · proof it works

Built to go slow — so every fellow finished with skills they can actually use.

Cohort 1 was kept deliberately small. Not a mass rollout, but a first cohort deep enough for every fellow to learn, apply and be mentored through genuine NTD data work — the evidence that the model holds before it scales.
